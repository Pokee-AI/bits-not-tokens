# Experiment Brief v5.1: Is the v4 Result Robust?

**Changes from v5 (decisions on the implementer's review):** the probe window is a fixed absolute document range, wider and identical in every run, with replace-not-insert semantics; probe facts are excluded from `facts_stored` and from absorption curves; probe accuracy is logged on both main and branch weights; per-fact `first_seen` / `last_seen` are saved; cosine details and the 1/(lr x wd) conversion are pinned down.

Extends the existing `bits-not-tokens/` repo. Reuse everything from v4 (world, RAW distribution p, flattened distributions and tau solver, warm-up W, models S / M / L, evaluators, measurement procedure, recorded deviations). Read the whole file before writing code.

## 0. Purpose

v4 found that flattening repetition stores 2.3x to 5.1x more facts at equal tokens, that FLAT matches a count-only prediction, and that a stationary stream reaches a steady state set by exposure *rate* within a forgetting horizon of order 10^7 documents. Every run shared one optimizer recipe: constant learning rate, AdamW weight decay 0.1. Weight decay alone implies a forgetting timescale of about 1/(lr x wd) steps, which is uncomfortably close to the measured horizon.

v5 asks whether the v4 finding is a property of the learner or of that recipe, and puts confidence intervals on it. **This is characterization, not another premise test.** v4's verdicts stand whatever v5 shows.

## 2. Corpora

For each model size, two corpora at budget B = 300,000,000 tokens unless stated:

- **RAW** (i.i.d. from p, filler 16)
- **FLAT-sel**: FLAT at the level that v4 selected on seed 1 for that size's FLAT corpus type (P3-eligible, most facts stored). Use the same tau as in v4.

Part B also uses **CURATED-sel** (v4's selected CURATED level per size).

## 3. Retention probe (added to EVERY v5 run)

A direct measurement of forgetting, so the horizon is observed and not inferred.

- **Probe set:** 2,000 facts drawn with the world seed from popularity ranks 200,000 to 400,000. Their probability in the normal stream is set to zero and the rest renormalized.
- **Window:** a fixed absolute document range, identical in every v5 run regardless of budget or corpus: documents **3,200,000 to 4,270,000** (1,070,000 documents; roughly 30 to 40% of a B-budget RAW or FLAT run, earlier in CURATED and in the 2B / 4B runs). v4 showed exposure rate is what matters, so the probe's rate must be the same everywhere; a percent-of-run window would not give that.
- **Semantics:** each probe fact appears exactly 100 times inside the window. Probe documents **replace** ordinary draws at uniformly random positions in the window; they are not inserted, so document count and token budget are unchanged. Probe documents are about 19% of the window. That is a real perturbation of the window (the head loses about a fifth of its exposures for that stretch), identical across arms, so comparisons between arms hold; say so in the results.
- After the window the probe facts never appear again.
- **Accounting:** probe facts stay in the `n_k` and per-fact hit arrays with a `is_probe` flag, but are **excluded from `facts_stored`, `bits_stored`, and all absorption-curve bins** (their exposures are windowed, not stationary). They are reported only in `results/v5_probe.csv`.
- **Measurement:** probe top-1 accuracy on the main-run weights at window end, then every 500,000 documents until the run ends. At the normal measurement points log it twice, labelled: on the main-run weights at snapshot time (0.9 x D), which belongs to the retention curve, and on the cooldown-branch weights at D, which is comparable to `facts_stored`.
- Report the retention curve (accuracy versus documents since window end) and the **half-life**: documents until accuracy falls to half its window-end value ("not reached" if so). This is the horizon for a stored fact with no rehearsal, which is the quantity of interest.

Because of the probe, v4 baseline runs are NOT reused; baselines are rerun.

## 4. Part A: optimizer ablation

Settings (everything else as v4):

| Name | Learning rate | Weight decay | Schedule |
|---|---|---|---|
| base | frozen v4 LR | 0.1 | constant, with cooldown branches at measurement points |
| wd0 | frozen | 0 | as base |
| lr3 | frozen / 3 | 0.1 | as base |
| wd0_lr3 | frozen / 3 | 0 | as base |
| cosine | frozen (peak) | 0.1 | 100-step warmup, cosine decay to 10% of peak at B; no cooldown branches |

For `cosine`: 100-step linear warmup, then cosine decay to 10% of peak exactly at B. Only the final point is a proper measurement; intermediate points are evaluated on the main-run weights and flagged `mid_schedule=1` in the CSV. The final point is compared with `base`, whose final point is also annealed (through its cooldown branch), so the comparison is fair.

Run matrix:

- M: all 5 settings x {RAW, FLAT-sel} x seeds {1, 2} = 20 runs
- S and L: {base, wd0, cosine} x {RAW, FLAT-sel} x seeds {1, 2} = 12 runs each

44 runs. Do not re-tune anything; these are ablations, not new recipes.

## 7. Criteria (copy verbatim into CRITERIA_v5.md and commit before any run)

- **R1 (the effect survives the recipe).** For M, under every one of the five optimizer settings, FLAT-sel stores at least 1.5x the facts RAW stores (seed mean, final point), and FLAT-sel's `weighted_acc_p` is no more than 0.02 below RAW's. ROBUST if it holds for all five; otherwise list the settings where it fails. Report the same for S and L on their three settings.
- **R2 (steady state).** For M under `base`, FLAT-sel facts stored at 4B is within 15% of its value at B (seed mean). If it grows by more than 15%, report NOT A STEADY STATE and give the growth exponent in tokens.
- **R3 (what sets the horizon), descriptive.** Probe half-life per setting and size, next to the weight-decay timescale 1/(lr x wd) steps = 128/(lr x wd) documents (infinite for `wd0`). Absolute agreement is not expected, since one is a half-life and the other an e-folding time of the weights; what matters is the scaling across settings (for example, whether `lr3` lengthens the half-life by about 3x). State plainly whether half-life scales with 1/(lr x wd), with 1/lr alone, or with neither. For `wd0`, a finite half-life means forgetting comes from interference, not decay.
- **R4 (intervals), descriptive.** FLAT-sel / RAW and CURATED-sel / RAW ratios per size as mean and 95% t-interval over seeds 2 to 5 (3 degrees of freedom, so expect wide intervals), with seed 1 shown separately. If GPUs are idle at the end, seeds 6 and 7 may be added for all Part B cells at once, never selectively.
- **R5 (L's optimum), descriptive.** Facts stored and `weighted_acc_p` versus level for L including the two new levels; identify the best P3-eligible level per seed.
- Under `cosine`, also report facts stored per setting against `base`: does a decayed schedule retain more of what was seen earlier in the run? Compare the absorption curve's dependence on *when* a fact's exposures occurred (early third versus last third of the run), using the saved `first_seen` / `last_seen` arrays.
